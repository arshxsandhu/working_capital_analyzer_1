Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) Mathematical Framework

Standard GAAP & corporate finance methodologies used in this engine

Finance Reference

1. Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)

DSO = (Accounts Receivable / Annual Revenue) × 365

Measures the average lag between shipping a product or completing a service and receiving cash in the bank. High DSO indicates loose credit policies or slow invoicing workflows.

2. Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)

DIO = (Ending Inventory / COGS) × 365

Measures how long inventory stays in holding before conversion into customer sales. High DIO locks working capital into warehouse shelves and increases obsolescence risks.

3. Days Payable Outstanding (DPO)

DPO = (Accounts Payable / COGS) × 365

Measures how long the business takes to settle invoices with vendors. Extending DPO delays cash outflows, effectively allowing vendors to finance your working capital interest-free.

Net Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC):

CCC = DSO + DIO - DPO  |  Net Working Capital = AR + Inventory - AP

A lower or negative CCC means your business turns cash into inventory and back into cash rapidly, requiring less short-term debt and unlocking self-funded enterprise growth.
